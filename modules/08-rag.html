Module 08

Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)

⏱ ~12 min read

What you'll learn

  • The problem RAG solves: grounding answers in your own, current documents.
  • The retrieve-then-generate flow, step by step.
  • Why companies favor RAG — and why answers still need checking.

The problem: the model doesn't know your stuff

A base LLM only knows what was in its training data, frozen at its knowledge cutoff (Module 5). It has never seen your internal wiki, your latest policies, or last week's incident report. Ask it about them and it will either admit ignorance or — worse — guess plausibly.

Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) fixes this without retraining the model. The idea: when a question comes in, first retrieve the most relevant documents from your own collection, then hand them to the model as context so it can generate an answer grounded in that material.

The retrieve-then-generate flow

Concretely, a RAG system usually:

  1. Splits your documents into chunks and stores their embeddings in a vector database (done ahead of time).
  2. Takes the user's question and finds the most relevant chunks via semantic search (Module 7).
  3. Inserts those chunks into the prompt alongside the question.
  4. Asks the LLM to answer using that supplied context — ideally citing which passages it used.

IT analogy

RAG is an open-book exam. Instead of relying on what the model memorized, you hand it the relevant pages right before it answers. The retriever is the index that finds the right pages fast.

Why companies use RAG

  • Current & private knowledge. Answers can reflect your own, up-to-date documents the base model never saw.
  • No retraining. Update the document store anytime; the system retrieves the latest content. Far cheaper and faster than fine-tuning for changing information.
  • Traceability. Good RAG systems cite their sources, so users can verify and trust answers.
  • Tighter scope. Grounding in approved documents reduces (not eliminates) off-topic or invented answers.

RAG reduces errors — it doesn't guarantee correctness

Retrieval can surface the wrong passage, miss the best one, or pull from an outdated document. And even with the right text in front of it, the model can still misread or misattribute it. Treat citations as something to check, not blind proof. Garbage in, grounded-sounding garbage out.

Key takeaways

  • RAG grounds answers in your own documents by retrieving relevant text, then generating.
  • The flow is retrieve-then-generate, usually powered by vector search.
  • It keeps answers current without retraining and enables source citations.
  • It lowers but doesn't remove the risk of wrong answers — still verify.

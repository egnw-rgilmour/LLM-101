Tokens & Tokenization

⏱ ~12 min read

What you'll learn

  • What a token is and why models work in tokens, not letters or words.
  • How tokens connect to context limits and pricing.
  • A hands-on feel for tokenization via an interactive demo.

Models don't read words — they read tokens

Before an LLM can process your text, the text is chopped into pieces called tokens. A token is often a whole common word, but it can also be a piece of a word, a space, or a punctuation mark. The process of splitting text this way is called tokenization, and the model has a fixed vocabulary of tokens it learned during training.

Why not just use words? Splitting into sub-word pieces lets the model handle any text — rare words, typos, names, code, other languages — by combining smaller known pieces, instead of choking on words it has never seen. Common words like "the" are usually a single token; an unusual word like "tokenization" might split into several.

IT analogy

Tokens are the model's character encoding, a bit like how text is stored as bytes. Just as a single emoji can take several bytes, a single long word can take several tokens. The model always counts in tokens, even though you think in words.

Why tokens matter for cost and limits

Two practical consequences make tokens worth understanding:

  • Pricing. Most commercial LLM APIs bill per token — both the tokens you send (input) and the tokens the model generates (output). Longer prompts and longer answers cost more. Verbose, repetitive prompts quietly run up usage.
  • Context limits. Every model has a maximum context window measured in tokens (covered in Module 6). If your prompt plus the conversation exceeds it, something has to be dropped, summarized, or split. A long PDF can easily blow past the limit.

A handy rough guide for English: a token averages about ¾ of a word, so ~1,000 tokens is roughly 750 words. This varies a lot with language, code, and formatting — treat it as a ballpark, not a rule.

Key takeaways

  • Models process text as tokens (whole words or sub-word pieces), not raw letters.
  • Sub-word tokens let models handle rare or unseen text gracefully.
  • Tokens drive both pricing (per-token billing) and context limits.
  • Rough rule: ~1,000 tokens ≈ 750 English words — but it varies.
